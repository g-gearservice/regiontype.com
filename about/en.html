Learn Seoul's districts and neighborhoods by typing them

regiontype is a typing drill where you learn place names by typing the ones that appear on the map. You start with Seoul's 25 districts (gu), and picking one takes you into its administrative neighborhoods (dong). All together, that is 423 of them.

A place name sticks longer when your hands write it than when your eyes skim it. Reading stops at "I've seen that," but moving the letters across one by one forces you to hold the whole name in your head. And typing it on a map binds the name to a place. If the cursor sits at the western edge of Seoul while you type Gangseo-gu, then next time the name alone sends your eyes to that corner.

The courses are not random — they are a connected route. The district course starts at Gangseo-gu on the western edge, loops clockwise around the city, and ends at Seongbuk-gu. Inside a district, the neighborhood course runs the same way, moving from one neighbor to the next. The order itself is built to be the skeleton you hang the geography on.

Learn them alphabetically and Gangnam, Gangdong, Gangbuk and Gangseo pile up together until you can no longer tell them apart. They share the syllable gang, but Gangseo-gu is the far west and Gangdong-gu is the far east. Learn them in the order the map loops, and each name keeps its direction.

What makes an administrative dong different from a legal dong

Seoul counts its neighborhoods two different ways. That is why "how many dong are there in Seoul?" has two answers.

A legal dong (beopjeong-dong) is a name attached to the land. It is used in the property register and in lot-number addresses, and it is usually an old village name carried down from the Joseon period and frozen in place. Being a name for land, it hardly ever changes.

An administrative dong (haengjeong-dong) is a unit that divides people. The area served by a single community service center is one administrative dong. Because the line is drawn to match headcount, a dong splits when the population grows and merges with a neighbor when it shrinks. This is the one that matters when you register a change of address or walk into a service center.

The two do not line up one to one. One administrative dong may bundle several legal dong, and one legal dong may be split across several administrative dong. Jongno-gu's Jongno 1(il)-, 2(i)-, 3(sam)-, 4(sa)-ga-dong is the first kind — one service center covers the several legal dong from Jongno 1-ga through 4-ga. In the same district, Cheongun-Hyoja-dong is likewise one name bundling several legal dong, Cheongun-dong and Hyoja-dong among them.

The 423 that regiontype covers are the administrative ones. They are the units people actually move through, and their boundaries are drawn clearly on the map. Since administrative dong are added and removed as districts are reorganized, this number reflects the data the app uses.
